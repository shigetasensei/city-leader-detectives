📖 About This Lesson

For students and families

🕵️ Open the City Leader Detectives game

🎯

Purpose and goals

Purpose: Grade 3 students learn to find trustworthy information about the city where they live, and to ask good questions to the leaders who run it.

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Know (知る): Search online and notice the difficulties and cautions, such as ads, old information, and similar names.
  2. Think (考える): Tell apart a real quote, an AI summary, and a guess, and say where each one came from.
  3. Ask (聞く): Write questions that the online sources cannot answer, and ask the mayors.
🎯

What we are doing

We are learning how to check what AI says about three real city leaders:

  • Mayor Ryōsuke Takashima of Ashiya
  • Mayor Toshiro Ishii of Nishinomiya
  • Kobe City Higashinada Ward Mayor Kenichi Kudo
💬

What is a real quote?

The exact words a person said or wrote.

📝

What is an AI summary?

A short version of a longer text, written by a computer. Summaries can be helpful, but they can add things that were never said or leave out important parts. Some people call it an AI overview.

🔀

Why keep them apart?

A summary can sound just like a quote. If we mix them up, we might believe something the mayor never said. In this game, quotes and summaries look alike on purpose, so we practice opening the source and checking.

🌍

Why it matters

We see AI summaries every day. Asking "Where did this come from?" helps us stay careful, fair, and kind online.

🕵️

What students do

Read each card, open the clue link, then sort the questions into "Can find online" or "Only the leader can tell us." For Mayor Ishii, students also complete quests by finding clues on his official profile.

🔗

Where this comes from

This lesson connects to UNESCO's guide AI Answers. You Decide., published on 10 November 2026. Its main idea is that AI does not change the basic goal of media and information literacy. It changes the setting where those skills are used, so critical thinking still matters most: asking where information came from and whether to trust it.

🏠

Ask at home

"Which answers could we find online? Which could only the mayor tell us?"
