📍 Kobe City: How Decisions Are Made

🏛️ How Kobe Makes Decisions

A city is too big for everyone to vote on every idea. So residents elect two groups to decide for them:

  • 🧑‍💼 The mayor runs the city's work (schools, buses, water, care for older people) and makes the plans.
  • 🗳️ The City Assembly (市会) is a group of elected members. (Most Japanese cities call this 市議会, but Kobe calls it 市会.) It votes on the city's budget (how money is spent) and on ordinances (the city's own rules).
  • 🤔 Guess 1: Who votes on how Kobe spends its money (the budget)?

How an idea becomes a decision:

  1. 💡 An idea: residents, staff, or the mayor see a problem.
  2. 📝 A plan: the mayor's team writes a proposal, such as a new budget or rule.
  3. 🗣️ Talk: assembly members ask questions and discuss it in committees and meetings. Anyone can watch.
  4. ✅ Vote: the assembly votes yes or no.
  5. 🚀 Action: if yes, the mayor and city workers carry it out.
  • 🤔 Guess 2: In the five steps, who usually writes the first proposal?

The two groups check each other, so no one person decides everything. Residents also choose them by voting, and both serve four-year terms.

  • 🤔 Guess 3: A new rule gets a "no" vote. What happens?
  • 🤔 Guess 4: Why do the mayor and the Assembly check each other?
  • 🤔 Guess 5: How long is one term for a mayor or an Assembly member?

Kobe is a big city with nine wards, and each ward has its own office for local needs. The City Assembly has 65 members. Source: Guide to the Kobe City Assembly (English, City of Kobe)
